The next cannabis consumer: are we ready for an education-driven marketplace?

Author
Rick Wagaman, MS (MCST)Founder, Herbal IQ Education & Consulting (opens in a new tab)
Published
September 8, 2026
Updated
September 14, 2026
Reading time
4 min read

As I’ve been thinking about where the cannabis industry is headed, one thing keeps coming back to me: the next cannabis consumer may not look much like the consumer our industry has traditionally served.

For years, many people entering cannabis businesses already had some familiarity with the plant. They may have consumed cannabis before legalization, understood the effects of THC, or at least walked through the door with a general idea of what they wanted. But we need to prepare for a different kind of consumer: someone who may be older, wellness-focused, medically curious, taking prescription medications, helping a spouse or parent explore cannabis, or simply trying it for the first time.

Some may have been interested for years but stayed away because of stigma, legality, uncertainty, or simply not knowing where to begin.

When that person finally walks through the door, the first question probably will not be, “What’s your highest-THC product?” It may be something much simpler: “Can you help me understand this?” That question represents both an opportunity and a responsibility for everyone working in this industry.

Start with the person, not the product

One thing I’ve learned through cannabis education is how quickly we can overwhelm someone new to this space. We talk about cannabinoids, terpenes, percentages, milligrams, formulations and delivery methods because those terms have become familiar to us. Imagine hearing all of that for the first time.

The questions that matter most are often more basic:

  • What is this?
  • What might I reasonably expect?
  • How do I approach it responsibly?
  • How long could the effects last?
  • Where should I turn when my questions become medical?

Good cannabis education begins by meeting people where they are. Start with the person, not the product.

Education has boundaries and that builds trust

Retailers and customer-facing employees can play an important educational role without becoming healthcare providers. Helping someone understand product formats, cannabinoid content, labels, serving information, onset, duration, storage, and general responsible-use principles can be tremendously valuable.

But when someone asks, “What should I take for my medical condition?” or raises questions involving prescription medications, contraindications, or individualized treatment decisions, the conversation has crossed into medical guidance. This distinction matters: the U.S. Food and Drug Administration notes that CBD can interact with medications and recommends discussing its use with a healthcare provider (opens in a new tab). The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health also maintains an evidence-based overview of what is, and is not, known about cannabis and cannabinoids (opens in a new tab).

Knowing when to say, “That’s a good question to discuss with your healthcare professional,” should not be viewed as failing the customer. It demonstrates professionalism and builds credibility. We do not need to blur the boundaries between cannabis businesses, educators, and healthcare professionals. We need to build better bridges between them.

Education is also a business strategy

Education should not simply be viewed as another expense. When employees have reliable training and approved resources, they do not have to reinvent an answer every time a difficult question comes across the counter. When consumers understand what they are purchasing and have more realistic expectations, uncertainty can decrease. When a business is comfortable acknowledging both what it knows and what it does not know, trust can grow.

Products can be copied, and prices can be matched. Trust is much harder to duplicate.

Is your organization ready for the next cannabis consumer?

A practical place to start is asking whether your organization can confidently say yes to the following:

  • We can explain cannabis in plain language without assuming prior knowledge.
  • We can explain product differences, including formats, onset, duration, and serving information, without overwhelming people.
  • Our staff understands where consumer education ends, and medical guidance begins.
  • We have a clear process for referring medical questions to qualified healthcare professionals.
  • Consumers receive consistent information across employees, shifts, and locations.
  • Our educational materials help people understand their choices, not simply promote a sale.

Any box you cannot confidently check may reveal an opportunity to strengthen staff training, consumer communication or referral resources.

Turn the checklist into an operating practice

A readiness check creates the most value when it changes what happens at the counter. Consider building a simple three-part system:

Define what staff can explain. Create approved guidance for product formats, labels, cannabinoid content, onset, duration, storage, and general responsible use, consistent with the rules in your market.

Define when staff should pause. Give employees clear language for questions involving medical conditions, medications, contraindications, or individualized treatment decisions.

Build a referral path. Maintain vetted resources for healthcare questions and make sure staff knows how to use them. Review referral resources regularly, and never imply a clinical partnership that doesn't exist.

Businesses can also learn by tracking the questions customers ask most often. Those questions can reveal gaps in staff training, product information, signage, website FAQs, and post-purchase education. Listening is part of readiness.

The checklist is a starting point

That thinking led me to develop the Next Cannabis Consumer Readiness Checklist through Herbal IQ Education & Consulting (opens in a new tab) as part of the Cannabis Chamber of Commerce’s Next Cannabis Consumer: Education, Trust & Responsible Access Industry Insights conversation.

The checklist is not about assigning a score or pointing out what an organization is doing wrong. It is meant to start a useful conversation. Take a few minutes, work through it with your team, and pay attention to the boxes you cannot confidently check yet. An unchecked box is not necessarily a failure. It may show where the next opportunity to educate, improve, and build trust exists.

Explore Herbal IQ’s cannabis education resources and guides (opens in a new tab), and visit the Cannabis Chamber of Commerce Resource Center for additional industry insights, webinars and practical business resources.

As this industry matures, the businesses best prepared for the next consumer may not be the ones with the loudest claims or the largest menus. They may be the ones that can make a complicated marketplace feel understandable, responsible, and worthy of trust.
